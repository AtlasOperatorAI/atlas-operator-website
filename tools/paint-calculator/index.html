How to estimate paint for a room

Paint is easy to over- or under-buy because the label gives coverage per gallon, while rooms are measured in feet. The fix is to turn the room into one number, the paintable square footage, and divide it by the coverage on the can.

1. Wall area

Add up the length of every wall (the perimeter) and multiply by the wall height. For a rectangular room that's 2 × (length + width) × height. A 12 × 10 ft room with 8 ft walls has 2 × 22 × 8 = 352 sq ft of wall.

2. Subtract doors and windows

This calculator takes off 20 sq ft per door (a standard 3 ft × 6 ft 8 in door) and 15 sq ft per window (3 × 5 ft). If yours are much bigger, such as patio doors or picture windows, measure them and adjust the count. One door and two windows bring the example down to 352 − 20 − 30 = 302 sq ft.

3. Multiply by coats, divide by coverage

Most interior paints list roughly 350–400 sq ft per gallon for one coat on a smooth surface. Check your product's label and use that number. Two coats on 302 sq ft is 604 sq ft of painting. At 350 sq ft per gallon that's 604 ÷ 350 = 1.73 gallons, so buy 2 gallons.

Ceilings and primer

A flat ceiling's area is simply length × width (120 sq ft in the example). Turn on "Include ceiling" to add it, but remember ceiling paint is often a different product, so you may want to run the ceiling separately with its own coverage. Primer is usually one coat, and it covers about the same area per gallon as paint. Its estimate is shown separately.

When to expect lower coverage

  • Rough or textured walls (knockdown, stucco, block) soak up more paint. Lower the coverage number.
  • New drywall is porous until it's primed.
  • Big colour changes, such as dark to light or bright reds and yellows, may need an extra coat.
  • Rollers vs sprayers. Spraying loses some paint to overspray.

Trim, doors and accent walls

Trim and doors are usually painted in a different sheen, and sometimes a different colour, so estimate them separately. A quart often covers the trim in an average room. For an accent wall, paint only that wall's area: width × height minus any openings, times the coats, divided by coverage. A 12 ft wall at 8 ft high with two coats is 192 sq ft of painting, a little over half a gallon at 350 sq ft per gallon, so a gallon (or two quarts if the colour is sold that way) covers it with some left for touch-ups.

FAQ

How many gallons of paint do I need for a 12x10 room?

With 8 ft walls, one door and two windows, the net wall area is about 302 sq ft. Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon is 1.73 gallons, so buy 2 gallons.

How much does a gallon of paint cover?

Many interior paints list about 350–400 sq ft per gallon per coat on smooth, primed walls. Rough or porous surfaces get less. Use the number on your paint's label.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

Yes. A typical door is about 20 sq ft and a window about 15 sq ft. Subtract them from the wall area before dividing by coverage.

Do I need two coats?

Usually, especially when you're changing colour or painting new drywall. One coat can be enough when you repaint the same colour over a sound surface.
